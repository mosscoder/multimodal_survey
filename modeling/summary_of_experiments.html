Frozen DINOv3 probe for 8-species multilabel presence

Given a robot ground photo (ground_image) and its paired nadir drone crop (drone_image), predict which of 8 target plant species are present in the frame. Frozen DINOv3 backbones + a shallow probe, evaluated with a spatially-honest 5-fold cross-validation grouped by survey leg (no frame's ground ever appears in both a training and a validation fold). Per-class AP: mean-pool vs. top-1

Same run, same fold split, same features — only the pooling operator changes. This isolates exactly what top-k buys per species.

Top-1 pooling (best) Mean-pool, all 3,072 patches (baseline)

Common, large-bodied species (Poa, Lupinus, Balsamorhiza) were already solved and barely move. The mid-frequency forbs — Gaillardia (+0.24 AP) and Achillea (+0.17 AP) — are where mean-pooling was hiding real signal in a sea of background patches. Euphorbia virgata (110 positives, the rarest class) still tops out around 0.40 — a long-tail data problem, not a pooling problem.

What moved the needle

  • +0.03 to +0.10Top-k patch pooling (mean → top-1). The dominant finding: small-species signal is concentrated in a handful of the 3,072 ground patches; averaging all of them drowns it out.
  • +0.05 to +0.10Ground-image resolution, 224px → 768px. Small forbs are sub-patch at 224px — the model can't resolve them until given more pixels.
  • +0.02Linear + MLP ensemble. The two heads are complementary — linear wins the mid-frequency forbs and the rarest class, MLP wins the common species and F1 calibration.
  • n/aMLP regularization (dropout 0.2→0.4, hidden 512→256). Brought the MLP to AP parity with linear while keeping its F1 edge — the earlier MLP was simply overfitting the small per-fold training sets.
  • +0.004Horizontal-flip augmentation. Small and free, best on Achillea (+0.031) — don't expect more from image-space augmentation on frozen features.

What didn't work

  • ≈ 0Drone-image resolution / sub-cropping. Native 320px ≈ 224px ≈ 512px-upscaled. Cropping to plant scale made every species worse, monotonically — the drone branch contributes whole-crop context, not localizable plant detail.
  • ≈ 0CLS vs. CLS+patch-mean pooling on its own — within noise, aside from a free lift for grasses (Bromus, Sisymbrium).
  • stuckEuphorbia virgata specifically. Moved a little with both-pooling+linear and with top-k, but never decisively past ~0.40. Rarest class (110 positives), one CV fold holds only 3 — a long-tail data problem, not a feature problem.

Not done / next

The official held-out test split (outermost legs) was never extracted at the winning (top-k, 1024×768) configuration — every number above is 5-fold CV on train. That's the next concrete step before treating 0.766 as a generalization estimate.
  • Long-tail loss (asymmetric / focal) or per-class thresholds, targeted at Euphorbia virgata.
  • Combine top-k ground pooling with the linear+MLP ensemble and/or CLS-token concatenation — not yet tried together.
  • CV early-stopping selects each checkpoint on the fold it's then scored on — fine for comparing configs, mildly optimistic as an absolute estimate.
  • Buffered leg-fold CV, leave-one-strip-out CV, and a fixed site-2 validation split were scaffolded (fold_masks.py) but not carried through the full matrix.
